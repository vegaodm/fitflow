How insights work

What the sleep tab compares, when it stays quiet, and what a finding is not

Privacy Support How insights work

The short version. The app compares the nights where you tagged a factor against the nights where you did not, on one measure at a time. It only speaks when both groups are big enough and the difference is bigger than noise. It never claims a cause.

An insight is a comparison in your own log, not medical advice and not a diagnosis. If sleep is a persistent problem, talk to a clinician rather than an app.

What gets compared

Every night records four numbers, and each insight is about exactly one of them:

Measure What it is
Time fell asleep Bedtime as a point on one timeline, so 23:30 and 00:15 are 45 minutes apart rather than a day apart.
Time asleep Wake time minus bedtime, in minutes.
Wakings How many times you remember waking.
Energy Your own 1–5 rating of the morning.

Two kinds of finding

Presence

“On nights with caffeine within 2h of bed, you fell asleep 38 min later.”

Compares nights where the factor was tagged at all against nights where it was not.

Timing

“Exercise in the evening is followed by 22 min more sleep than exercise in the morning.”

Compares two of the four time buckets — morning, afternoon, evening, within 2h of bed — for the same factor. This is the more useful one, and it needs more nights to be trustworthy.

When the app stays quiet

A comparison is only shown when all of these hold:

  1. Enough nights on both sides. Free users need 3 nights with the factor and 3 without, and 5 nights in total. With Lifetime Membership it is 5 and 5, and 14 in total.
  2. A difference worth mentioning. 15 minutes for bedtime and duration, half a waking for wakings, 0.3 points for energy. Below that the app shows nothing rather than a number too small to act on.
  3. The window is long enough. A paid user looking at a 7-day window still gets the stricter free gate, because 14 nights cannot fit in a week.

So an empty Insights tab after four nights is the app working correctly, not failing. Five nights is the first useful comparison; two weeks is where timing findings become worth reading.

Why findings are ranked the way they are

Insights are ordered by a standardised effect size with a small-sample penalty: a finding from 4 nights against 4 is shrunk toward zero more than the same difference from 20 against 20. That keeps a dramatic-looking fluke from sitting at the top of the list.

The card always prints the two averages and the number of nights behind each, so you can judge the evidence yourself rather than trusting a ranking.

How to log well

  • Tag the same factors consistently. If you tag caffeine some nights and forget on others, the “without caffeine” group is polluted and the comparison weakens.
  • Log the morning you wake, not later. Duration and bedtime are exact; the energy rating is a memory, and memories drift by the afternoon.
  • Two weeks beats two months of gaps. Consecutive nights are worth more than a long, sparse history.
  • Change one thing at a time. If you cut caffeine and start exercising in the same week, the app cannot tell you which one mattered — and neither can the data.

The optional AI brief

The Insights screen can hand a plain-text summary of your last two weeks to an AI assistant you choose. The summary contains your dates, sleep times, duration, wakings, energy ratings, factor tags and any note you wrote.

  • It is sent only when you tap the button, and only to the app you pick.
  • Spendly FitFlow itself sends nothing and stores no API key — see the privacy policy.
  • Once handed over, the receiving service's terms apply.
  • Notes are included. Do not write anything in a sleep note you would not want a third party to read.

What an insight is not

  • It is not a diagnosis, and it is not a substitute for a sleep study.
  • It is not proof of cause. Two things moving together in one person's log can share a third cause — a stressful week moves caffeine, alcohol, bedtime and energy at once.
  • It is not comparable to anyone else's numbers. The gates, the baselines and the buckets are all yours.
  • It is not a medical device. Nothing here measures sleep stages, breathing or oxygen.

If you snore heavily, wake gasping, or feel sleepy while driving, see a doctor. An app that asks you to tap “Start” when you get into bed cannot see any of that.
