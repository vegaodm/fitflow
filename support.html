Getting started

The app opens on Food. There are three tabs:

  • Food (left) — today's diary, the calorie ring and the food library.
  • Running (middle) — record a route, see pace, splits and elevation.
  • Sleep (right) — log a night and see what moves it.

Settings is the gear at the right end of the top bar on the Food tab; Stats sits at the other end. It is not a fourth tab: the three tab slots belong to the three features.

First thing to do

Open Settings → Goals and set your sex, birth year, height and weight. Those four numbers are what turn a run into calories and a goal into a daily target. Until they are set, the app uses a neutral default and every number will be off.

Free records and Lifetime Membership

The app is free to download with a shared budget of eight records. A record is one new row: a meal, a logged exercise, a weigh-in, a run, or a night. The eight are shared across all three features, and the Food tab shows how many are left.

  • Editing an existing record does not spend the budget.
  • The 152-item reference food library and your personal targets are free and do not count.
  • Lifetime Membership is a one-time purchase, not a subscription. It removes the cap and unlocks the week/month/year statistics, the database backup and the longer sleep insight windows.

Restoring a purchase

Settings → Membership → Restore purchases. This also appears on the paywall. If nothing is found, make sure you are signed in to the same Apple Account you bought with.

If the price will not load

The paywall needs a connection the first time so Apple can return the local price. If it shows a fallback price or an error, check your connection and reopen the screen.

Backup and restore

Settings → Backup writes the whole database — meals, runs and nights in one SQLite file — to a location you pick in the Files app. Restoring replaces the current database with that file, so back up before you restore something old.

The running tab still has no import of its own — one path over the shared file is enough. There is one rescue hatch for the old app, and it lives here: your RunningAir runs can be imported from Settings → Backup. The next section has the steps.

Your photos and voice notes are separate files under Files → On My iPhone → FitFlow → FitFlow. A database backup does not include them, so copy that folder as well if you want them.

Importing your runs from RunningAir

Runs recorded in RunningAir can be brought across. In RunningAir, tap the share icon in the top bar (Export Database) and save the file somewhere you can reach. Then in Spendly FitFlow open Settings → Backup → Import RunningAir runs and pick that running.sqlite.

Only runs are copied: the route, its points and the post-run notes. Your meals, your nights and your profile stay exactly as they are, and a run you already have is skipped — picking the same file twice writes nothing twice.

A recording that was never finished is not a run, so leftover ones are counted and not imported.

Imported runs carry no calorie figure. RunningAir never measured one, and this app will not invent one from today's profile.

The import sits inside Settings → Backup, so it is part of the Lifetime Membership like the rest of that screen. Nothing is uploaded: the file you pick is read on your device, and a file that is not a RunningAir database is refused with a message.

Exporting runs, and what the Running top bar does

The Running tab's top bar carries one icon at each end, and neither is a word. The calendar on the left opens the monthly statistics: the month grid, one row per month, and the personal-reference panel. The export arrow on the right is the whole export, in a single control.

To export runs, tap the arrow, pick the runs you want — tap a row, or swipe it to the right — then tap the arrow again. The share sheet opens with one GPX file per run, ready for Files, a message or another app. Done leaves the selection without exporting, and Delete removes the runs you picked.

A run with no usable location sample has no route to write, so it produces no file. Record outdoors with Precise Location on and there will be one.

Why a run did not change my calorie budget

A run only counts once it has finished and has at least two track points. If it was discarded for having no usable route, no calories are written.

If the number looks wrong, check these in order:

  1. Settings → Goals — weight, height, age and sex. The estimate is per kilogram of body weight, so a stale weight shifts every run.
  2. Whether the run actually finished, in the Running tab.
  3. Whether the run's day is the day you are looking at. A run started before midnight belongs to the day it started.

Calories are frozen when a run ends. Editing your weight later does not rewrite past runs — that is deliberate, so history stays auditable.

How burned calories are worked out

Two kinds of burned energy appear in the Exercise sheet, and they are produced in completely different ways. Both count towards the same day's budget.

Manual exercise — you type the number

Add exercise asks for a name, a duration and the calories burned. The app stores exactly what you enter and does no arithmetic. It has no way to know how hard a swim or a gym session was, so guessing would be worse than asking.

A finished run — calculated from the route

When a run stops, its energy is worked out once and written to the database with the profile it used. It is never typed, and never recalculated later.

1 · MET — average moving speed, looked up in the Compendium of Physical Activities table.
2 · Gross cost — MET × 3.5 × weight in kg ÷ 200 × moving minutes. This is the ACSM running equation.
3 · Age and sex — your basal rate divided by that of a 30-year-old of the same height and weight, clamped between 0.90 and 1.10.
4 · Result — the product, rounded.

A 70 kg, 30-year-old male running 5 km in 30 minutes averages 10 km/h, which is 10.1 MET. That gives 10.1 × 3.5 × 70 ÷ 200 × 30 = 370 kcal, and the age-and-sex factor is 1.00 because he is the reference person.

Tap any run in the Exercise sheet to see this working for that run, step by step, along with the weight, age and sex it used.

Those three values are copied from your profile when the run ends. Changing your weight afterwards does not rewrite past runs — the history stays as it happened. Editing your weight does change the next run, and your daily target, immediately.

The figure is gross, not net of resting metabolism. That is the same convention as the daily target, so the two can be subtracted from each other without a correction nobody would remember to apply.

Location and background recording

  • Allow Precise Location. With it off, iOS reports roughly 5 km accuracy and no route can be drawn.
  • Allow Always if you want the route to keep recording with the screen locked or the app in the background.
  • If the status shows Searching for a long time, you are indoors or in a street canyon. Step outside; the recorder keeps trying.
  • Low Power Mode can throttle location updates. For a long run, turn it off.

The widgets

Long-press the Home Screen → + → search for Spendly FitFlow → pick a widget. Three are available, and none of them opens the database: each draws what the app last wrote into the shared container.

  • Bedtime Tap Start when you get into bed and Stop when you wake; Add details opens the night's form. The app imports the night the next time you open it.
  • Add meal Shows what is left of today's calories and how many meals are logged. Tapping it opens the meal form.
  • Run controls Start opens the app, because only the app can begin a GPS session. While a run is recording, Pause and Finish work straight from the Home Screen; once it is paused, those buttons open the app instead, where you can resume or finish. The small size keeps the clock and two glyph buttons; the medium one spells both out.

Long-press the app icon for Add meal — it opens the same form as the widget.

  • If a widget says “Not tracking”, the App Group capability is missing on an unsigned build. Install a signed build from the App Store or Xcode.
  • A session running longer than 16 hours is closed automatically at the cap rather than saved as a 30-hour night.
  • A night shorter than an hour is rejected, and the app tells you why.
  • A run card that keeps counting after the app was force-quit is stale: the recorder is what publishes it, so opening the app corrects it.

Delete data

  • One record — swipe it in the app.
  • Everything — Settings → Data → Delete all data. Meals, exercise, weigh-ins, runs, nights, photos, recordings and custom foods go. The reference food library and your personal targets stay.
  • The app — deleting Spendly FitFlow deletes its storage, including the database and all media.

Nothing is stored anywhere else, so there is nothing to ask us to delete.

Language, units and formatting

Settings → App → Language switches between System, English, Deutsch, Français and Español without a relaunch. Dates and decimal separators follow the chosen language, not the device.

Running distances and pace follow the metric/imperial switch on the Running tab.

Something looks wrong

The app will not open

You will see a screen saying the database could not be opened. Your existing data has not been deleted. Free some storage and reopen; if it persists, email us with the message shown.

Numbers look impossible

Check Settings → Goals first — a mistyped birth year or weight propagates into every target and estimate. The Goals screen shows the BMR and TDEE it derived, so the error is usually visible there.

Diagnostics

Settings → Data → Share diagnostics produces a plain-text log of the current month. It contains no personal data beyond what you typed into the app, and it never leaves the device unless you share it yourself.
